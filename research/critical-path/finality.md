# Causal finality timing

## Mixed-contract trace at 100 ms proposal cadence

This follow-up resolves the post-preparation remainder in the [critical-path report](README.md#critical-path-wall-time). A timing-only build of `eeefe97` recorded proposal, vote, quorum and commit events under exact epoch, view and Header digest. All 55,000 signed transactions and 267 certified blocks independently replayed. Its 2,122.84 common TPS, 95.61 validator CPU seconds and 1.402 s write-to-common p99 were within the spread of recent uninstrumented mixed controls. These comparisons check for obvious probe distortion and do not isolate a performance effect.

Each measured non-empty committed parent names its actual certified child in the recorded finality proof path. We joined 100 such parent and child pairs with all four local durable commits. The three matched Follower records per view use each validator's first corresponding proposal ingress and handler before preparation. Times below are medians and p95s of separate populations. Their column totals do not represent a single observed path.

The two preparation rounds and the child Leader's proposal interval take the largest parts of the node-side path. The observer adds another median 220 ms. That interval contains status sampling, RPC queueing, receipt of all four status responses and evidence assembly. It is part of observed client visibility. The measured 0.2 ms loopback transport delay and 14.4 ms per-replica finality commit are too small to explain the entire gap on this fixture. A controlled cadence comparison can test the 100 ms pacing cost while holding execution and durability fixed.

Within the 25-second offer, every timed successful preparation matched a committed block by digest, comprising 102 Leader and 303 Follower preparations. The Leader and Follower elapsed totals were 2.663 and 27.309 aggregate seconds across replicas. This run had zero repeated preparation of an identical point by the same replica, zero successful preparations superseded by a different canonical point at that height, and zero reported replica errors or transport drops. Repeated proposal envelopes that did not retrigger execution were excluded from per-Follower timing. The result covers the mixed test on a local healthy network.

`CLOCK_MONOTONIC` is shared by all node processes on this Linux host. Input enqueue timestamps come from the existing transport control timestamp paired with the current monotonic clock. The earliest collector QC is used for each point. The last `commit_visible` on the four replica threads establishes durable visibility. The observer comes from the independent driver's common-height ledger. The clocks and observer translation are same-host only. The trace has fresh RocksDB stores and the same parameters and signed corpus as the [mixed profile](README.md#measurement-and-reproduction). ## Matched 100 ms and 50 ms mixed finality

The same timing binary ran the fresh mixed workload in 100/50/50/100 ms order. Only `proposal_interval_ms` changed. Both configurations held the 2,400 ms timeout, block limits, two VM workers, storage options, signer keys and workload constant. The two plans generated byte-identical genesis files with SHA-256 `f7dc961e8b998ad32ecd44297bd9f46b15e51c1692e6f5280ea0f3a04a7da24a`. Every arm acknowledged and finalized all 55,000 signed transactions. Independent verification replayed 1,415 blocks across the four histories.

| Proposal cadence | Common TPS | Write-to-common p99 | Median proposal to durable on all replicas | p95 proposal to durable | Validator CPU | WAL syncs |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 ms A1 | 2,122.84 | 1.402 s | 389.6 ms | 452.1 ms | 95.61 s | 3,183 |
| 50 ms B1 | 2,144.24 | 1.190 s | 248.8 ms | 298.8 ms | 102.21 s | 5,317 |
| 50 ms B2 | 2,121.76 | 1.257 s | 247.6 ms | 324.4 ms | 100.71 s | 5,288 |
| 100 ms A2 | 2,107.92 | 1.828 s | 412.5 ms | 521.9 ms | 98.86 s | 3,010 |

The faster cadence reduced median durable finality by 36.1% and 40.0% in the matched pairs. Child view entry to construction fell from 105–106 ms to approximately 55 ms. Non-empty blocks moved from 536–546 median payments to 318–327, reducing per-block Follower preparation. No observed offer-window block reached the item cap. Shared visibility p99 improved in both pairings, with a median 198–226 ms observer gap still present in the faster arms.

Average validator CPU rose 4.3%. WAL sync count increased 71.2% and aggregate WAL bytes rose 6.2% over the whole offer and drain. The first faster trial recorded 128 peer-handler rejections during the offer, while the other three runs recorded zero. The count does not identify the message type. All four arms had zero local view timeouts and drained completely. The causal trace matched 100, 166, 160 and 93 fully observed parent and child pairs in run order. These single-host measurements support 50 ms as a mixed-workload latency option if the sync budget fits. Keep the existing 100 ms reference for payment and default comparisons. The fixed 2,200/s arrivals cannot establish a capacity advantage.

Reproduce by following the same [driver setup](README.md#measurement-and-reproduction), preparing the mixed topology again with `proposal_interval_ms` set to `50`, and measuring and replaying all four arms from fresh directories. The maximum block resources and the consensus, storage and checkpoint formats are shared. The RocksDB sync figures are before/after increments summed over all replicas. The CPU and TPS figures use the offer window.

The 180-second measurement followed the usual 60-second warmup. It matched 336 non-empty parent and certified-child pairs to all four local durable commits. All 819 observed blocks and 720,000 signed payments independently replayed. The diagnostic run measured 855.57 aggregate validator CPU seconds and 4.556 s submission p99. No successful preparation was superseded by a different candidate or repeated on the same replica. This count covers completed preparation only. It leaves requests that failed before that boundary in their own groups. The tracing job recorded no local view timeout. Although the pacing floor remains present, sustained Follower preparation is about 2.5 times as long as that floor. CPU and state access remain the higher-leverage targets here.

The prefunded corpus ultimately finalized in full. A missing Cell while checking a relay is consistent with a late or queued repeat after the spend. These counters establish the time and cause at validation. They do not establish the message's exact network-arrival order relative to the commit. The near-boundary difference from 89,846 generic peer-handler errors is consistent with the different sampling windows and possible other peer message classes. Admission totals flush at each durable commit, so the measured window has commit-sized boundary uncertainty. The useful replicated VM work far exceeds the combined CPU spent on the measured duplicate and missing-Cell outcomes.
